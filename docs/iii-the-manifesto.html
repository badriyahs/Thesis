The Manifesto

Prior to embarking on this project, I had a set of beliefs that I knew were the pillars of what I wanted to eventually undertake.

  1. the ‘gap’. we know how we started and where we are now, but thousands of years of iteration is neglected by the way we learn.

  2. environmental logic is intuitive. a child could do it, and children did do it. we’ve just unlearned it. all you need is your body. your fingers, your shoulders, your arm.

  3. we have no real sense of scale. or time, or duration, or distance. we imagine and feel these things through tools and screens, forgetting their context. globalization makes it more and more foreign.

  4. orientation is everything. all that you learn and will produce in your life relies on how you know what time it is, and where you are in the world. everything. this is why it’s important to define and be careful in imagining time and space.

  5. knowledge is disappearing. more knowledge is completely lost than we could ever comprehend. it is being rewritten and skewed at a terrifying, accelerating rate. and even what manages to survive, our minds are no longer able to receive. so it exists, but it is incomprehensible to us. so it has not survived.

  6. humans are not that creative. the attributes of the things we imagine rely on the attributes of the tools or the language we use to represent them.

  7. we design tools that flatten and erase. there is a way to be efficient, and advanced, without erasure. we can cultivate the field without clearing it.

  8. we set our own traps. the tools we design have invisible constraints which we assume are reality. and the actual phenomena that set their framework are forced into an artificial symmetry. the artificial symmetry becomes reality.

  9. our psychology is built on time and space being an active, physical, embodied, bodily discovery, not a passive reception of a number.

  10. look up. looking up at the sky’s ambiguity allowed our minds to wander into the study of time, math, philosophy, religions, and all of what makes us feel human, like vulnerability, reassurance, and wonder. we need to learn how to look up again.
